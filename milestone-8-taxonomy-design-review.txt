Website Classification Architecture and Taxonomy Design Review
===============================================================

No files or records were modified as part of the review.

1. CURRENT STATE
----------------

The application currently has one category dimension with three seeded categories:

- education — Education — Children policy
- entertainment — Entertainment — Teen policy
- social — Social — Teen policy

This single category currently carries semantic classification, age-policy selection,
reporting, and some review behavior.

Relevant current behavior:

- WebsiteClassification has one required category and age policy per row.
- ClassificationRun represents processing lifecycle: pending, running, completed,
  failed, and cancelled.
- Infrastructure safe-exclusions are controlled non-consumer run results with no
  consumer classification or age policy.
- UT1 is conservative: education can short-circuit; social and audio/video are
  evidence-only; adult and gambling are high-risk/manual-review evidence.
- AI output is restricted to database categories and cannot set age policy.
- Current rules fall back toward education. This should eventually become an
  explicit uncategorized/manual-review outcome, not a semantic classification.

2. RECOMMENDED MULTIDIMENSIONAL MODEL
-------------------------------------

Keep processing status, content, service scope, security reputation, and policy
decisions separate.

ClassificationRun
  └─ ClassificationAssessment
       ├─ primary content label
       ├─ secondary content labels
       ├─ scope/service labels
       ├─ security/reputation labels
       ├─ terminal disposition
       ├─ evidence/provenance
       └─ policy decision snapshot

Recommended entities:

- taxonomy_versions: immutable, checksum-protected published taxonomy snapshots.
- taxonomy_labels: versioned labels with dimension, slug, display name,
  definition, status, and defaults.
- classification_assessments: one normalized assessment per run/website, with
  terminal disposition and primary content label.
- classification_assessment_labels: multi-label associations with dimension,
  confidence, source, role, and bounded evidence.
- assessment_policy_decisions: derived policy snapshot containing age/rating,
  blocked/review-required, policy version, and reasons.
- feed_label_mappings: versioned mappings from UT1/feed labels to taxonomy labels
  and handling mode.

Dimensions:

- Content: what the website substantively provides. One primary label and zero or
  more secondary labels.
- Security: malware, phishing, fraud, or other threat signals. May coexist with
  content.
- Scope/service type: CDN, DNS, cloud hosting, messaging, proxy, infrastructure,
  and similar roles. Never implies a consumer category.
- Age-policy recommendation: derived from confirmed content labels and
  administrator policy; never supplied by UT1 or AI directly.
- Terminal disposition: classified, uncategorized, non-consumer infrastructure,
  unreachable, safety-blocked, review-required, failed, or cancelled.

4. AGE-POLICY MATRIX
--------------------

- education-reference: preserve existing Children mapping for legacy records. Do
  not assume all future reference material is child-directed.
- entertainment-streaming, social-networking, gaming: Teen default.
- adult-content, gambling, drugs-controlled-substances, weapons-explosives:
  Adult, with manual review and jurisdiction-sensitive blocking policy.
- illegal-content-piracy: no ordinary age recommendation; review/block policy
  takes precedence.
- malware-threats and phishing-fraud: no age policy; security block
  recommendation is distinct from age suitability.
- CDN, cloud, messaging, proxy, and non-consumer infrastructure: age policy is
  not applicable.
- Other ordinary content labels: General/no minimum age by default.
- Uncategorized, failed, and unreachable: no age policy; never infer safe.

5. THREAT AND BLOCKING MATRIX
-----------------------------

- malware-threats: critical threat; block recommended; review required.
- phishing-fraud: critical threat; block recommended; review required.
- illegal-content-piracy: high risk; block recommended after confirmation;
  review required.
- proxies-anonymizers: contextual risk; organization policy decides; usually
  reviewed.
- adult-content, gambling, drugs-controlled-substances, weapons-explosives:
  content risks; policy decides blocking; review required.
- Scope-only infrastructure: no threat and no block by itself.

A block recommendation is not enforcement. The policy engine remains the sole
authority for final blocked status.

6. CONFLICT AND PRECEDENCE RULES
--------------------------------

1. Processing state is separate from classification. Failed, cancelled, and
   safety-blocked runs are never silently converted to content categories.
2. Safe non-consumer infrastructure is terminal: scope evidence may be stored,
   but no consumer content category or age policy exists.
3. Security labels coexist with content labels. Malware/phishing are never
   overwritten by content, and their block/review recommendation has precedence.
4. Select one primary content label only when evidence thresholds are met;
   additional confirmed content labels are secondary.
5. Scope labels cannot become consumer categories. CDN and cloud services are
   not substitutes for technology-software.
6. High-risk or strong-source conflicts require manual review.
7. Manual locked decisions override automated output. AI may recommend content
   only and cannot set policy, security disposition, or taxonomy.
8. Uncategorized means no sufficiently reliable classification; it is never
   safe and is never education.

7. MINIMAL DATABASE CHANGES
---------------------------

Use additive changes only. Do not modify or delete current categories,
age policies, WebsiteClassification rows, or UUIDs.

Proposed migrations:

- 0007_taxonomy_dimensions.py
  Add taxonomy versions, taxonomy labels, assessments, assessment-label
  associations, policy decision snapshots, enums, and indexes. No production
  classification behavior changes.

- 0008_taxonomy_legacy_backfill.py
  After approval, seed a published taxonomy version and project legacy education,
  entertainment, and social records into assessments.

- Later migration
  Add versioned feed mappings and evaluator v2 fields only when the feed/category
  rollout begins.

Key constraints:

- taxonomy_version_id + slug is unique.
- assessment_id + label_id is unique.
- At most one primary content label exists per assessment.
- Scope/security labels cannot be primary content.
- Policy decision is nullable for scope-only, infrastructure, unknown, failed,
  and cancelled outcomes.
- Published taxonomy versions are immutable; corrections create a successor with
  lineage and checksum.

8. API CHANGES
--------------

Use additive v2 fields and preserve all existing response fields.

Add an optional assessment object to website/classification responses:

- primary_content
- secondary_content
- security_labels
- scope_labels
- terminal_status
- policy_decision
- taxonomy_version
- bounded evidence provenance

Retain category_id, category history, and age-policy response fields throughout
the compatibility period.

Future administrative operations should publish taxonomy versions and mappings
through audited operations, not arbitrary live category mutation. Future manual
review may accept dimensioned labels and a policy decision while retaining the
current single-category override as a compatibility path.

9. FRONTEND CHANGES
-------------------

No initial redesign is needed. Later, add distinct text-rendered badge groups:

- Content
- Security
- Scope/service type
- Age-policy decision
- Processing outcome

Keep the existing category list as a legacy view until a taxonomy-version UI is
ready. Show infrastructure as Non-consumer infrastructure, not as a consumer
category. Render label names and evidence as inert text.

10. CLASSIFIER, FEED, BROWSER, AND AI FIT
-----------------------------------------

- classification_service.py: produce separate content, scope, and security
  conclusions; eventually remove the education fallback.
- Rules: version weights by taxonomy version and dimension. Never produce threat
  or blocking labels from weak keywords alone.
- UT1: use versioned mappings. Education may be final; social/audio-video are
  evidence-only; shopping/games unsupported evidence; adult/gambling high-risk
  review evidence.
- Infrastructure detector: retain exact-domain-only safe exclusions. Emit scope
  and terminal non-consumer disposition, rather than a consumer category.
- HTTP/browser: feed bounded evidence into the same assessment with source
  provenance. Browser cannot bypass policy checks.
- AI: recommend active content labels only. It must not create security blocks,
  scope exclusions, policies, or categories.

11. MIGRATION AND BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY
----------------------------------------

- Preserve all current categories, policies, WebsiteClassification rows, and IDs.
- Legacy mappings:
  education -> education-reference
  entertainment -> entertainment-streaming
  social -> social-networking
- Continue serving legacy API fields from the existing projection during the
  transition.
- Do not retroactively infer security/scope labels unless existing evidence
  proves them.
- Existing immutable evaluation datasets remain valid under a recorded legacy
  taxonomy version. New evaluations use a new version.

12. EVALUATION PLAN USING EXISTING PILOT DATA
---------------------------------------------

Use completed 100-domain pilots only as offline evidence; do not reprocess public
domains.

1. Create a derived immutable evaluation dataset from existing runs and manual
   review.
2. Label outcomes as consumer content, confirmed non-consumer infrastructure,
   controlled limit/unreachable, safety rejection, or ambiguous/manual review.
3. Measure primary/secondary content accuracy, scope precision, security false
   negatives, policy accuracy, false exclusions, source usage, and cost/time.
4. Require human adjudication before using disagreements as ground truth.
5. Compare taxonomy versions rather than mixing metrics across them.

13. CONSERVATIVE ROLLOUT
------------------------

Phase 1: Data model only. Add additive taxonomy/version/assessment structures
without pipeline changes.

Phase 2: Publish initial taxonomy and backfill only the three legacy categories.

Phase 3: Move existing exact infrastructure outcomes into the scope/disposition
model without adding detector rules.

Phase 4: Add a small set of high-confidence content labels with deterministic
rules and evaluation fixtures.

Phase 5: Roll out versioned UT1 feed mappings while retaining current
conservative behavior.

Phase 6: Extend manual review and AI recommendations to dimensioned content
labels. AI remains policy-blind.

Phase 7: Calibration, pilot replay, independent category/policy metrics, and
carefully gated wider rollout.

14. RISKS AND FALSE-POSITIVE AREAS
----------------------------------

- Education/reference is broader than child-directed education; blanket Children
  policy can over-restrict or under-restrict.
- Adult, gambling, drugs, weapons, piracy, and proxy signals vary by jurisdiction
  and context; weak text/feed evidence must not auto-block.
- Finance and shopping can share terms with fraud.
- Cloud/CDN/hosting/messaging providers serve both machine endpoints and
  consumer-facing content; only exact high-confidence endpoints should exclude.
- UT1 data can be stale, broad, or unsuitable as sole authority.
- Browser availability and bot mitigation can bias results toward failures.
- AI can hallucinate or be prompt-injected; it should stay bounded, validated,
  content-only, and non-authoritative for policy.
- Taxonomy changes can invalidate comparisons unless every assessment, ruleset,
  feed mapping, and policy decision records its version.

15. SAFEST MINIMAL FIRST IMPLEMENTATION PHASE
---------------------------------------------

Phase 1 should add only the versioned multidimensional storage model and its
invariants. It should not change the classification pipeline, current category
behavior, feeds, browser behavior, AI behavior, pilot selection, or existing API
responses.

Estimated Phase 1 touch points:

- backend/alembic/versions/0007_taxonomy_dimensions.py
- backend/app/models.py
- backend/app/taxonomy.py
- backend/tests/test_taxonomy_models.py
- backend/tests/test_taxonomy_versioning.py
- docs/taxonomy-model.md

No frontend, router, classifier, browser, AI, pilot, or feed changes are required
in Phase 1.
